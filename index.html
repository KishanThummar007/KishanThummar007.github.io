My work focuses on robotics simulation and motion planning for automotive wire harness handling. My academic background combines mechanical engineering with Electromobility-ACES at FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg. My current interests include reinforcement learning, world models and robotic manipulation of flexible objects.

For my master’s thesis at FAPS, I built a MuJoCo simulation and developed a trajectory planner for loading a wire harness into a multi-gripper. The planner uses simulated wire stress and a collision-proximity penalty to refine trajectories. I also co-authored a benchmark of learned models and physics simulators for predicting cable motion. My earlier project thesis used MVTec HALCON to estimate the position and orientation of connector sockets from 3D point clouds.

    World Models for Deformable Linear Objects: Benchmarking Physics-Based and Learned Approaches for Wire Harness Manufacturing

    Research manuscript · CIRP CMS 2027

    A benchmark of six learned world and dynamics models and four physics simulators on a near-industry wire handling task, comparing how they represent and predict cable motion and physical properties such as stiffness and rest shape.
